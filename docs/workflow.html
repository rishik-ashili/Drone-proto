Drone-proto workflow

frame: local NED, metres from takeoff · x north · y east · z down · no GNSS

We are building a quadcopter that flies a route with no GPS. It measures its own motion with optical flow, the IMU and later stereo VIO. It recognises known landmarks with a YOLO network and ArUco tags, and uses each sighting to cancel the drift. This page shows what we build, in what order, with which models and algorithms, and where each step stands today.

2 · What happens in flight, 20 times a second

In-flight loop: sensors to motors

The companion computer (laptop, Raspberry Pi, later the Jetson) runs perception, drift correction, safety and the mission. PX4 on the Pixhawk runs the estimator and the flight control. The same code drives the simulator, PX4 SITL and the real drone.

3 · How the landmark AI is made

AI pipeline: synthetic data to an engine on the drone

The simulator renders and labels its own training images, so labels are exact and free. Public data broadens the helipad class. Real photos of the printed landmarks are the next loop, shown dashed.

4 · How the drone decides what to do

Mission logic: the state machine

Each state streams Offboard setpoints to PX4 from the GPS-free estimate. The health monitor can take over from any flying state. The pilot can always take control in Position mode.

5 · From simulation to the airframe

Verification ladder

Every capability climbs the same ladder (plan section 10): simulation, replay, PX4 SITL, bench with props off, tethered, then free flight. Each rung names the tool that runs it and what it proves.
